# Student GenAI Evidence Matrix — Initial Targeted Review

This targeted review was performed on 2026-11-13 to establish an initial external evidence boundary for the Student Impact research protocol. It is **not a systematic review**, does not validate the synthetic dataset or its burnout labels, and does not establish that AI causes burnout.

## Search and selection boundary

Primary full-text methods/results were inspected for three contrasting AI-and-learning studies: one randomized immediate-learning experiment and two higher-education observational studies. A second bounded search targeted validated student-burnout instruments and longitudinal undergraduate evidence. Searches included `generative AI learning randomized`, `AI tutor randomized`, `ChatGPT students survey`, `student burnout inventory validation`, `CBI-SS validation`, `MBI-SS validation`, and related higher-education records.

Selection retained positive, null and adverse-associated results plus four peer-reviewed measurement studies. Publisher access constrained the AI-and-learning slice toward preprints. Supplementary analyses were not independently reproduced. The foundational MBI-SS record was checked from publisher-deposited abstract metadata rather than full text; the three other measurement records had accessible primary full text.

## Synthesis for this project

The studies are compatible rather than contradictory once intervention and outcome are separated:

1. Targeted dialogue can improve **immediate** conceptual performance in a specific randomized setting.
2. Binary self-reported AI use may show no independent adjusted association with authentic essay scores.
3. AI-susceptible task acceleration may coexist with lower **unaided retention** in observational platform data.

Therefore the project must not compress AI use into a universally beneficial or harmful exposure. Future interpretation should distinguish tool availability, instructional scaffold, task purpose, engagement quality, assisted performance, unaided retention, academic outcomes and well-being measures.

None of the studies validates `Burnout_Risk_Level`, the synthetic dependency/anxiety fields, individual risk probabilities or a causal GenAI-to-burnout pathway. The measurement studies provide candidate methods for future real-student research, not a mapping from synthetic 1–10 fields to validated scales. May et al. adds three-semester burnout-profile evidence but does not establish an AI exposure pathway or broad long-term trajectory.

For a future external-validation protocol, Wongtrakul et al. is the strongest procedural template in this bounded slice because it combines translation/adaptation, pilot work, CFA, convergent/discriminant evidence and test–retest reliability. It is **not** permission to reuse its Thai 22-item scoring unchanged. The target language and population would require a prespecified version, cognitive pilot, psychometric checks, test–retest interval and external criteria.

Validated AI-dependency measurement, peer-reviewed longitudinal GenAI/well-being studies, cross-cultural invariance and external validation of the synthetic label remain evidence gaps. AI exposure, anxiety, dependency, burnout and academic outcomes must remain separate constructs.

## Problematic GenAI use and dependency candidates

No candidate in this bounded search is ready for direct adoption as a validated higher-education GenAI-dependency instrument. The records below support exploratory pilot design only.

| Study | Population and construct | Validation evidence | Protocol decision and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Yu, Chen, and Yang (2024), [*Development and Validation the Problematic ChatGPT Use Scale: A Preliminary Report*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-024-06259-z), peer-reviewed | 1,040 Taiwanese adults spanning undergraduate, graduate and working groups; exact student proportions were unavailable. Eleven symptom-style items address preoccupation, withdrawal, tolerance, loss of control, displacement, continued use despite problems, sleep loss, deception and mood modification. | Reported EFA/CFA supported one factor, with published loadings .693–.881. The abstract reports internal consistency and test–retest reliability, but numerical coefficients, retest interval and complete sampling details were inaccessible in this review. | Most construct-relevant candidate because it measures problematic ChatGPT use, but it lacks dedicated student validation, longitudinal outcomes, predictive validity and cross-cultural equivalence. Visible items do not establish unrestricted reuse rights; platform-specific wording may age quickly. |
| Maral et al. (2026; online 2025), [*Problematic ChatGPT Use Scale: AI-Human Collaboration or Unraveling the Dark Side of ChatGPT*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11469-025-01509-y), peer-reviewed, open access | Turkish Study I *n*=391 adults, 25.1% students; Study II *n*=473 adults. Adaptation of Yu's eleven-item scale. | Items 1 and 4 were removed after low loadings (.246 and .245), leaving a nine-item unidimensional form. The study reports CFA, item-total and IRT evidence plus configural/metric/scalar gender invariance; no test–retest study was reported. | Adds cross-cultural construct evidence, not a university-specific validation. Changed item retention weakens score equivalence; convenience adult samples, cross-sectional correlates and no external behavioral criterion limit adoption. Full items are available under CC BY 4.0. |
| Morales-García et al. (2024), [*Development and Validation of a Scale for Dependence on Artificial Intelligence in University Students*](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2024.1323898), peer-reviewed, open access | 528 Peruvian medical students aged 18–37; EFA *n*=226 and CFA *n*=302. Five items cover feeling unprotected without AI, falling behind, relevance/image, AI validation and skill-replacement fear. | One-factor EFA explained 65%; reported CFA CFI=.99, TLI=.98, RMSEA=.05 and SRMR=.02; α=ω=.87 with strict gender invariance. | Direct student sample and CC BY 4.0 item availability make it pilotable, but its content mixes dependency with performance anxiety, social relevance and replacement fear. One medical-school context, no convergent/discriminant or criterion validation, no retest and no longitudinal validity prevent direct adoption. |

**Protocol decision:** keep GenAI dependency exploratory. Before any real-student use, define the intended construct, cognitively pilot candidate items, verify reuse rights, run CFA and convergent/discriminant checks, add behavioral criteria and test–retest evidence, and test invariance across disciplines and languages. Do not combine the score with burnout or anxiety and do not create a risk cutoff.

## Citation status and known discrepancies

- All three entries were inspected from primary arXiv full text, not abstract-only summaries. As of the search date, peer-reviewed publication was not independently verified for Weijers or Rismanchian.
- Shakil reports forthcoming conference/DOI metadata; treat the inspected artifact as a 2026 preprint until publication status is independently confirmed.
- Weijers' abstract enrollment of 165 must not be reported as the analyzed sample of 141.
- Shakil's 1,894 is a survey-response total, not unique participants; overview grade-link counts (316/342) and ANCOVA counts (333/343) differ.
- Rismanchian's 0.75 odds multiplier means 25% lower odds under the model, not a 25-percentage-point decrease.
- The Schaufeli entry is abstract-verified in this review; detailed numeric psychometric claims beyond the deposited abstract were not admitted.
- Wongtrakul and Oluwadiya removed different CBI-SS items in different cultural samples; neither modified form should be treated as a universal scoring key.
- May's three-semester profile stability is longitudinal association, not evidence that AI use caused burnout.
- The original PCUS numerical reliability details and retest interval were inaccessible beyond abstract/publisher tables; do not quote unavailable coefficients.
- The Turkish PCUS adaptation removed two original items, so the nine- and eleven-item forms are not assumed interchangeable.
- The five-item DAI record is student-specific but blends dependency with anxiety and obsolescence concerns; its name alone does not establish construct specificity.
